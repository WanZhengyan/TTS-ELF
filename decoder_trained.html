Was The Decode Head Trained

Yes, in the codec runs — and it worked. ELF's decode head was trained in every codec-target run and reached the codec's own quality ceiling. No, in the latest mel run — there I switched it off on purpose, because a mel spectrogram has nothing discrete to decode into.

How I know, rather than assuming

I checked the two saved checkpoints directly. A head that never receives a gradient keeps its initial values; a trained one moves.

Rununembed_kernel std(init scale)unembed_bias maxVerdict
codec — decoder_prob 0.21.034~0.0443.179trained — 23× its init scale, bias well away from zero

The bias is the decisive one. It starts at zero, and any gradient at all would move it. On the codec path it reached 3.18; on the mel path it is still exactly 0.000000 after 100,000 steps.

One correction to my own check: I first flagged the mel head as "trained" because its EMA copy differed from the raw copy by 3×10⁻⁵. That is floating-point drift from applying p ← 0.9999·p + 0.0001·p a hundred thousand times to two identical tensors, not learning. The threshold I used was too tight; the bias is the reliable signal.

Why this matters more than it sounds

The decode head is the one ELF-specific mechanism that demonstrably works on audio. Given a correct latent, it reproduced the codec's own quality exactly — teacher-forced WER 5.27 against a ceiling of 5.44 — and it beat plain nearest-codebook rounding by 63 percentage points at the noise level it trains on. The part that failed was the flow feeding it, not the head.

So the mel run, by switching the head off, gave up the one piece that was working in order to fix the piece that was not. That is a reasonable trade for getting audible speech out, but it is a trade.

It also suggests the obvious next build: let the flow generate mel, where it demonstrably works, and then put ELF's decode head back on the end to map mel to codec codes — a mapping already shown to reach the ceiling. Two parts, each in the space it is good at.
